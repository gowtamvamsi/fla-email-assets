// AI Engineer vs ML Engineer
Two different jobs.
One confusing title.
Why most AI Engineer roles never ask you to train a model, and what they test you on instead.
PYTHON · RAG · MCP · LANGGRAPH · EVALS · GUARDRAILS · DEPLOY
Hi {{ name }},
“I am in confusion, some people say ML is required for AI engineering.”
I’ve received some version of that message more times than I can count this year. So here’s the short answer, side by side.
ML EngineerAI Engineer
Main jobTrains and tunes modelsBuilds products on top of existing models
Daily toolsPyTorch, GPUs, training pipelinesPython, LLM APIs, RAG, agents, LangGraph
The hard partGetting a model to learnMaking a system accurate, safe and affordable in production
Needs firstStrong maths and statisticsSolid Python and engineering habits
Many job posts still say “AI/ML” because they were copied from older templates. Read the responsibilities and ignore the title.
If the work is LLMs, RAG, agents and evaluation, it’s an AI Engineering role, and you won’t be quizzed on backpropagation.
// NOTE
A 30-second test for any job description
Count how many of these appear: LLM, RAG, agents, LangChain, LangGraph, vector database, prompt engineering, evaluation.

Three or more? That’s AI Engineering. Mostly PyTorch, model training and feature engineering? That’s ML, and a different path.
AI Engineering is where most of the new roles are being created right now. It’s open to anyone who can write good Python and think like an engineer, whether you’re in college or 15 years into your career.
